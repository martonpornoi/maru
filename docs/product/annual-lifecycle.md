# Annual event lifecycle

Status: Baseline  
Last updated: 2026-07-26

Maru treats an event edition as a lifecycle, not a weekend. Dates are relative
guidance; each organizer configures its own cadence.

## Permanent organization layer

The organization persists across editions and owns:

- legal and governance records;
- convention series and brand history;
- people, alumni, skills, and organization memberships;
- reusable policies, role definitions, forms, templates, and automations;
- suppliers, venues, assets, storage locations, and long-lived contracts;
- chart-of-accounts mappings and finance integrations;
- integration credentials and communication channels;
- knowledge base, risk register patterns, and retention policies;
- cross-edition metrics and lessons.

Event editions copy or reference approved organization templates while keeping
their operational state independent.

## Phase 1: Strategy and mandate

Typical timing: 18–12 months before live event

Questions:

- Should this edition happen?
- What is its purpose, scale, audience, and risk appetite?
- Who is accountable?
- What changed since the prior edition?

Capabilities:

- edition proposal and charter;
- target dates, size, budget envelope, theme, and success measures;
- venue and date scenario comparison;
- prior-edition lessons and unresolved actions;
- preliminary risk, accessibility, safeguarding, and technical assessments;
- board or director approval and decision record.

Gate: edition authorized with accountable leadership and planning assumptions.

## Phase 2: Venue, finance, and operating structure

Typical timing: 15–10 months before live event

Capabilities:

- venue, hotel, room-block, and supplier pipeline;
- site visits, floor plans, capacities, access zones, power and network notes;
- contract deliverables, deadlines, insurance, permits, and cancellation terms;
- edition budget, cost centers, approval limits, cash-flow forecast;
- department structure, leads, on-call ownership, and vacancies;
- high-level programme, stage, Dealers' Den, art, charity, food, and transport
  capacity;
- dependencies and critical-path milestones.

Gate: contracted or viable venue plan, approved budget baseline, department
ownership, and critical risks assigned.

## Phase 3: Team formation and service design

Typical timing: 12–8 months before live event

Capabilities:

- staff recruitment, interviews, offers, onboarding, training, and access;
- service catalog: what each department provides, when, where, and to whom;
- policies, escalation routes, support categories, and response expectations;
- ticket, lodging, merchandise, dealer, sponsor, guest, and volunteer product
  design;
- application and review workflows;
- communications calendar and brand asset production;
- technical architecture and integration readiness.

Gate: each critical service has an owner, operating design, staffing plan, data
needs, and published dependencies.

## Phase 4: Sales, applications, and commitments

Typical timing: 9–4 months before live event

Capabilities:

- registration launch, fair queue or lottery, waitlists, payments, transfers;
- lodging applications, roomshares, accessible allocation, and inventory;
- programme, performer, dealer, art, charity, press, sponsor, and volunteer
  submissions;
- juried and blinded review where configured;
- guest travel, agreements, technical riders, and hospitality;
- demand forecasts and capacity adjustments;
- automatic acknowledgements, deadlines, reminders, and team inbox routing.

Gate: demand is understood, commitments are traceable, and exceptions have
owners.

## Phase 5: Detailed planning and readiness

Typical timing: 5 months to 2 weeks before live event

Capabilities:

- accepted programme and versioned timetable;
- workforce demand model, shifts, qualifications, rest, and coverage;
- run-of-show, rehearsals, stage plots, cue sheets, and technical riders;
- room layouts, signage, access zones, capacity, and queue plans;
- badge, credential, meal, merchandise, and print production;
- supplier call times, load-in/out, deliveries, vehicles, and storage;
- emergency, evacuation, accessibility, medical, awareness, and incident plans;
- equipment kits, radios, keys, cash floats, handover packs, and checklists;
- data imports, integration rehearsal, printer/scanner testing, and offline
  bundles;
- tabletop exercise and failure simulation.

Gate: critical readiness evidence accepted. Red risks require explicit director
  acceptance or remediation.

## Phase 6: Arrival, setup, and go-live

Typical timing: 7 days before public opening through opening

Capabilities:

- staff, supplier, dealer, guest, and equipment arrival board;
- load-in routes, dock slots, rooms, keys, credentials, and handovers;
- venue condition and safety checks;
- live staffing gaps and call-sheet acknowledgements;
- registration desks, kiosks, printers, scanners, signage, network, and relay
  health;
- command-center clock, on-call roster, and escalation tree;
- controlled transition from planning state into live operations.

Gate: doors may open only after required safety, access, staffing, and service
checks.

## Phase 7: Live operations

Typical timing: convention days

The primary interface switches into **Now mode**:

- what is happening now and next;
- people or resources not ready;
- uncovered or late shifts;
- room, queue, capacity, accessibility, and service status;
- incidents, requests, lost property, and maintenance;
- change proposals and their impact;
- announcement and acknowledgement state;
- integration, device, printer, signage, and network health;
- financial and inventory exceptions;
- director log and shift handovers.

Planning detail remains available, but the interface prioritizes time-critical
decisions and accountable action.

## Phase 8: Breakdown and immediate close

Typical timing: final event day through 2 weeks after

Capabilities:

- load-out, returns, damages, stock counts, key and radio reconciliation;
- lost-and-found chain of custody;
- volunteer hours, missed shifts, rewards, and welfare follow-up;
- supplier completion and disputed deliverables;
- auction, art, merchandise, dealer, and charity settlement;
- refunds, chargebacks, invoices, expenses, and reimbursement queues;
- incident follow-up and evidence preservation;
- attendee and staff feedback;
- data correction window and preliminary operational report.

Gate: physical and financial custody reconciled; urgent welfare and incident
actions assigned.

## Phase 9: Learning, retention, and archive

Typical timing: 2 weeks to 6 months after

Capabilities:

- department retrospectives tied to evidence and outcomes;
- success measures, attendance, capacity, finance, staffing, programme,
  accessibility, communication, and incident analysis;
- action owners and due dates that can carry into the next edition;
- policy, template, checklist, and training updates;
- participant history and recognition;
- retention, deletion, anonymization, and legal holds;
- final schedule, content, public archive, and approved reports;
- edition closure and read-only archival checkpoint.

Gate: archive approved, retention jobs scheduled, lessons assigned, and future
edition template changes reviewed.

## Readiness model

Readiness is evidence-based, not a manually chosen percentage.

Each service or workstream defines:

- accountable owner and backup;
- required deliverables;
- dependencies;
- target and hard deadlines;
- current risk and confidence;
- evidence of completion;
- rehearsal or validation result;
- go-live and rollback criteria.

Maru derives readiness signals:

- **Ready:** required evidence accepted and dependencies satisfied.
- **At risk:** forecast miss, weak evidence, unresolved dependency, or capacity
  concern.
- **Blocked:** required dependency unavailable.
- **Not assessed:** owner or evidence missing.
- **Accepted risk:** accountable director explicitly accepts a documented gap.

A green dashboard cannot be produced by hiding overdue work.

## Edition cloning

Creating next year's edition is a reviewed carry-forward:

1. Select source edition and organization template version.
2. Preview copied configuration and excluded operational data.
3. Import accepted retrospective actions.
4. Reset dates, owners, prices, capacities, credentials, and external IDs.
5. Flag stale policies, suppliers, integrations, and unverified assumptions.
6. Require owners to re-accept inherited responsibilities.

Blind duplication is prohibited because it preserves old mistakes as silently
as it preserves good practice.

# Personas and jobs to be done

Status: Baseline  
Last updated: 2026-07-26

Maru is not designed around database tables or existing department boundaries.
It is designed around decisions that people need to make under changing
conditions. One person may inhabit several personas during the same edition.

## Shared expectations

Every persona should be able to:

- use one account and understand which organization and edition is active;
- see the actions, deadlines, conversations, and changes relevant to them;
- understand why information is requested and who may see it;
- move from a notification to the affected object and next action;
- avoid re-entering information already supplied for the same purpose;
- receive a useful denial instead of a mysterious missing page;
- work with localized times, language, accessibility, and communication
  preferences; and
- distinguish an official decision from discussion, draft, or rumor.

## Attendee

**Jobs**

- Decide whether the event is suitable, affordable, and accessible.
- Register, pay, select options, and resolve issues without chasing staff.
- Keep a reliable pass, personal schedule, messages, and venue information.
- Understand changes while protecting personal boundaries.
- Retrieve receipts and a meaningful participation history later.

**Failure today**

The attendee copies the same details into registration, hotel, volunteer,
programme, and dealer forms; watches several social networks; and cannot tell
whether a message is official.

**Successful experience**

`My Maru` presents the next action, current registration state, personal
timetable, unread official changes, tickets or credentials, and only those
applications the attendee actually uses.

## First-time attendee

**Jobs**

- Understand unfamiliar terminology, norms, age boundaries, and venue layout.
- Know what to bring, where to arrive, and where to ask for help.
- Discover welcoming activities without being overwhelmed.

**Successful experience**

An edition-specific journey explains milestones in plain language. A
personalized arrival checklist, orientation programme, quiet-space information,
and help entry point are available without exposing the person as a first-timer.

## Host, speaker, performer, or guest

**Jobs**

- Propose or accept an appearance and provide only relevant details.
- Communicate availability, access needs, technical riders, and travel needs.
- Review the public description and know the final room, call time, contacts,
  and deliverables.
- Receive and acknowledge changes.

**Successful experience**

One appearance record connects the accepted proposal, people, travel,
accommodation, consented biography and media, technical needs, schedule,
rehearsal, backstage access, expenses, and communications without making every
department see every field.

## Volunteer

**Jobs**

- Discover roles that fit interests, qualifications, access needs, and energy.
- Compare shifts against programme, travel, required breaks, and commitments.
- Complete onboarding and know where to report.
- Check in, ask for help, record completion, and understand earned benefits.
- build a portable, accurate contribution history.

**Failure today**

The volunteer joins several chats, receives a spreadsheet row without context,
and learns about a shift change from another volunteer.

**Successful experience**

The volunteer sees a single action queue, recommended suitable shifts, schedule
conflicts before committing, a shift briefing, reporting location, escalation
contact, and a private record of completion.

## Staff member

**Jobs**

- Understand responsibilities, deadlines, authority, and dependencies.
- Complete required policy, training, and access steps.
- Execute work without learning every module.
- Handover decisions and unfinished work professionally.

**Successful experience**

The staff home shows assigned work, messages, upcoming commitments, approvals,
blocked dependencies, and the smallest set of tools required for the role.

## Department lead

**Jobs**

- Design a service, recruit and onboard a team, and prove readiness.
- Plan coverage, assets, spaces, budget, dependencies, and failure modes.
- Coordinate with other departments without duplicating trackers.
- Run the department live and leave an intelligible handover.

**Successful experience**

A department cockpit combines its plan, people, roster, tasks, risks, assets,
requests, inbox, budget position, readiness evidence, live status, and
post-event actions.

## Director or board member

**Jobs**

- Set mandate, risk appetite, budget envelope, and decision authority.
- See whether the event is genuinely ready without micromanaging.
- Resolve cross-department tradeoffs and maintain accountable decisions.
- Meet legal, financial, safeguarding, and governance obligations.
- Compare editions without treating misleading numbers as truth.

**Successful experience**

The portfolio view emphasizes evidence-backed readiness, accepted risks,
forecast variance, critical dependencies, unresolved decisions, and material
changes rather than vanity metrics.

## HR and volunteer coordination

**Jobs**

- Attract, evaluate, onboard, support, reassign, and offboard people fairly.
- Match staffing demand to skills, availability, and development goals.
- Track agreements and training without turning personnel data into gossip.
- Handle sensitive cases with explicit separation and accountable access.

**Successful experience**

HR has configurable pipelines, structured but humane reviews, qualification and
availability views, role-based onboarding, access reconciliation, workload
signals, and limited case workspaces.

## Registration and Front Desk

**Jobs**

- Help a person quickly even when their description is incomplete.
- Explain and correct registration, order, entitlement, credential, or hotel
  state under clear authority.
- Check in people and issue physical items reliably under venue conditions.
- Record an exception so the next staff member understands it.

**Successful experience**

Fast identity resolution leads to a consolidated service view with masked
sensitive data, reasoned actions, eligibility checks, payment references,
fulfilment, prior contacts, and explicit escalation.

## Programme curator and scheduler

**Jobs**

- Collect proposals, review consistently, and communicate decisions.
- Transform accepted content into a coherent programme.
- Reconcile people, rooms, resources, access needs, safety boundaries, audience
  flow, and publication deadlines.
- Understand who and what is affected before changing a published schedule.

**Successful experience**

Programme review and scheduling share one source of truth but have role-specific
views. Every publication is versioned and every material change has an impact
preview.

## Dealer, artist, or merchandise vendor

**Jobs**

- Apply, provide portfolio and business information, and understand the rules.
- Select or accept space, equipment, power, assistants, and fulfilment options.
- Receive decisions, invoices, setup instructions, and live support.
- Participate in future editions without unnecessary re-entry.

**Successful experience**

A guided application becomes a controlled commercial relationship connecting
review, contract, space, credentials, order, setup slot, compliance, support
case, and retained organizer history.

## Stage, AV, and production crew

**Jobs**

- Turn programme promises into an executable technical plan.
- Collect riders, designs, files, rehearsals, cue sheets, and approvals.
- Know call times, access, equipment, dependencies, and last-minute changes.
- Log faults, substitutions, show notes, and handovers during live operation.

**Successful experience**

The production view connects each show to a room configuration, technical
advance, crew plan, assets, cue/run sheet, rehearsal, recording consent,
communications, and incident timeline.

## Logistics, venue, and hospitality

**Jobs**

- Coordinate spaces, keys, loading, vehicles, storage, assets, suppliers,
  catering, travel, accommodation, and deliveries.
- Know custody and location, not just expected quantity.
- Handle changes without losing the dependency chain.

**Successful experience**

Time and place are first-class: movements, bookings, handovers, and constraints
feed the same operational schedule and readiness model used by other teams.

## Finance, procurement, and sponsorship

**Jobs**

- Build and control budgets, commitments, purchases, reimbursements, sales,
  settlements, and restricted funds.
- Obtain approvals and evidence without chasing private messages.
- Reconcile operational facts with the accounting system.
- Track sponsor benefits and obligations.

**Successful experience**

Maru provides operational subledgers and approval workflows, attaches costs and
contracts to the work they enable, and exports clean records to the authoritative
accounting system. It does not pretend to be a statutory ledger.

## Security, awareness, medical, and safeguarding

**Jobs**

- Receive concerns through discoverable and discreet channels.
- Triage, coordinate, act, and hand over with minimum necessary disclosure.
- Track location, welfare, evidence custody, actions, and follow-up.
- Operate a known emergency plan when Maru or the network is unavailable.

**Successful experience**

Cases live in separated workspaces with explicit duty roles, break-glass
controls, access review, redacted handover, retention policy, and an immutable
access trail. Immediate safety never depends on software authorization.

## Accessibility coordinator

**Jobs**

- Publish accurate access information and collect actionable requests.
- Coordinate accommodations across registration, hotel, rooms, programme,
  staffing, and evacuation planning.
- Share only the operational consequence, not an unnecessary diagnosis.

**Successful experience**

One restricted request can produce scoped tasks such as “reserve step-free
seating” or “provide a quiet check-in” without exposing the underlying personal
information to task assignees.

## Communications and content

**Jobs**

- Plan content, collect approvals, localize, publish, and measure delivery.
- Maintain one canonical event fact across web, app, email, social, signage,
  press, and printed materials.
- Communicate urgently without bypassing accountability.

**Successful experience**

A content item records source facts, audience, variants, owners, approval,
schedule, delivery outcomes, and corrections. A room change can update every
eligible destination from one reviewed change set.

## IT and platform operations

**Jobs**

- Provision integrations and access safely.
- Diagnose user and system problems without unbounded database access.
- Monitor capacity, delivery, security, backups, and venue connectivity.
- recover service and reconcile offline activity.

**Successful experience**

Support tooling exposes explainable permission decisions, correlation IDs,
redacted event timelines, connector health, job replay, access review, and
tested recovery procedures.

## Supplier, venue contact, or temporary collaborator

**Jobs**

- Supply exactly the documents, confirmations, availability, and live updates
  requested.
- View only the relevant schedule, task, location, or asset handover.
- Avoid joining the convention's entire collaboration environment.

**Successful experience**

Expiring guest access or a secure request link provides a narrow, branded
workspace with explicit purpose and auditability.

## Charity and art show teams

**Jobs**

- Intake objects with provenance, permissions, condition, reserve, and
  beneficiary.
- Manage display, custody, bids, settlement, collection, and reconciliation.
- Separate donor, artist, buyer, and public information appropriately.

**Successful experience**

Every physical item has an accountable chain of custody and state transition.
Public results, payment handling, and organizer records derive from that source
without publishing personal or commercially sensitive data.

## Cross-persona design test

A feature is incomplete if it helps the department creating a record but not:

1. the person asked to supply information;
2. the next department depending on it;
3. the live operator who must act on it;
4. the auditor or successor who must understand it later; and
5. the subject who needs visibility, correction, export, or deletion rights.

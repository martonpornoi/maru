# Key end-to-end workflows

Status: Baseline plus executable Workforce-only adoption
Last updated: 2026-08-26

These narratives test whether modules form a convention operating system rather
than a collection of forms. Detailed acceptance scenarios will refine them.

## Start with Workforce only

1. A platform administrator opens **Set up Workforce** and reuses the highest
   Organization → Convention series level already present.
2. They enter only missing organization/convention identity plus the edition
   name, dates, and time zone. Maru stores `workforce_only@1`, `en`, and the
   internal no-currency sentinel in one idempotent transaction.
3. Maru reuses the existing accountable representation or provisions truthful
   Maru operators. Two distinct people accept their own invitations before a
   platform administrator activates the organization.
4. Operators define Structure. If the organization has no compatible Position
   template, one controller creates the code-owned safe Volunteer starter with
   a different controller's approval; the starter grants nobody authority and
   creates no person or attendee state. Operators then create Positions,
   connect purpose-bounded volunteer accounts, independently approve
   assignments, collect deliberately shared Availability, and plan and confirm
   Shifts.
5. Incumbent attendee, payment, attendance, programme, communications, venue,
   and logistics systems continue unchanged. Maru creates no Participation,
   Registration, payment, or attendance state and grants no related authority.
6. If the organization later wants another Maru workflow, it makes a separate
   reviewed adoption decision; Workforce authority does not expand itself.

**Proof of success:** a volunteer can be assigned and scheduled, and operators
can complete the full Workforce journey, while the same edition remains absent
from Registration discovery and contains no attendee Participation or payment
state.

## 1. Create and authorize a new edition

1. A director creates the edition from a series and selects a prior edition or
   approved template as a source.
2. Maru inventories copyable policy, forms, products, roles, schedule
   structures, spaces, vendors, documents, automations, and reports.
3. Every selected item receives an owner and `review required` state; stale
   dates, people, prices, credentials, contracts, and regulated text are never
   silently activated.
4. Leadership sets mandate, dates, venue, currencies, languages, budget
   envelope, decision authority, and initial milestones.
5. Department leads accept responsibility and review inherited assumptions.
6. The edition moves from Draft to Preparing only when required governance and
   privacy ownership are present.

**Proof of success:** a new edition is useful immediately but contains no hidden
promise that something copied from last year remains true.

## 2. Recruit and onboard a staff member

1. A department publishes a role with outcomes, workload, skills, schedule
   expectations, access, and support.
2. The candidate applies using a platform account and chooses which existing
   profile information to share.
3. Reviewers use a rubric, declare conflicts, request information in the
   application thread, and record a decision.
4. An accepted application records its typed transition evidence and makes the
   person eligible for the purpose-bounded Assignment candidate set. It creates
   no RoleAssignment, capability, or Participation evidence.
5. An authorized controller separately proposes the Position assignment, and a
   genuinely different current controller completes stepped-up approval. Only
   that approval activates the scoped RoleAssignment and, when the immutable
   edition profile adopts Participation, its configured evidence.
   Workforce-only creates no attendee Participation; neither profile grants
   permanent blanket access.
6. The role template generates training, agreement, profile, equipment,
   meeting, and access tasks with owners and deadlines.
7. Provisioning adapters create approved external access and report actual
   state back to Maru.
8. The member and lead see readiness and blockers. Sensitive HR information
   remains outside the department cockpit.
9. A role change recalculates tasks and access; offboarding revokes access,
   recovers assets, transfers ownership, and preserves an authorized historical
   record.

**Proof of success:** no spreadsheet is required to answer who is ready, who can
access what, or what remains after a person leaves.

## 3. Register, pay, allocate lodging, and arrive

1. The organizer activates a reviewed, immutable edition form with an overall
   period, capacity, privacy purposes, and separate volunteer, early-bird, and
   normal-price products.
2. The attendee uses the bundled reference form or a seasonal API client and
   sees authoritative availability, price, deadline consequence, cancellation
   terms, accessibility information, and data purpose.
3. A returning attendee may review a clearly sourced prior-profile suggestion.
   Submission creates an independent edition profile; public-list consent is
   deliberately chosen again and older conventions do not change.
4. Conditional questions collect only relevant information. Restricted offers
   use server-owned capacities or eligibility facts; self-entered text cannot
   grant a discount.
5. An available paid place becomes a time-limited reservation. A full eligible
   offer may join a no-payment FIFO waitlist when policy allows.
6. A payment provider handles card data; Maru verifies an authenticated result
   against local amount and currency, reconciles it idempotently, and records
   the resulting entitlement.
7. Expiry releases capacity. Automatic waitlist promotion offers the oldest
   eligible person a fresh payment window and stops when the applicable sale
   period closes.
8. If demand exceeds another scarce resource, an explainable allocation round
   handles hotel, room type, or entitlement requests under versioned policy.
9. Room-group invitations and roommate consent form a complete group without
   publishing personal contact information.
10. Current-edition profile changes and public-list withdrawal are self-service
    and leave the immutable registration submission intact. Transfers, refunds,
    deadlines, waivers, and other exceptions use
   reasoned workflows and produce an attendee-visible timeline.
11. Before arrival, the attendee receives a personalized checklist and QR or
    alternative lookup credential.
12. Front Desk resolves identity, eligibility, payment, waiver, accommodation
    tasks, credential, and fulfilment from one service view.
13. Offline check-in records signed operations locally and reconciles conflicts
    when connectivity returns.

**Proof of success:** the attendee and desk agent agree on the current state,
finance can distinguish provider payment, free admission, and waiver, and every
physical or financial exception is explainable.

## 4. Take a programme item from proposal to publication

1. A call for participation defines tracks, formats, questions, review rubric,
   content boundaries, consent, accessibility, and decision dates.
2. A submitter creates a proposal, adds co-hosts, availability, public content,
   and technical or access needs with field-level visibility.
3. Curators perform staged, optionally anonymized review, manage conflicts of
   interest, communicate revisions, and make an accountable decision.
4. Acceptance creates a programme item, host commitments, advance tasks, and
   unpublished scheduling demand.
5. The planner places it using room capability, capacity, host availability,
   audience flow, setup/teardown, recording consent, staffing, and equipment.
6. The host reviews public copy, call time, location, and material needs.
7. A schedule release is approved and atomically updates public programme,
   personal schedules, calendars, signage source, staff views, and APIs.
8. Later changes preview impacts and target affected people for acknowledgement.
9. After delivery, show reports, attendance observations, files, and host
   contribution feed the archive under retention policy.

**Proof of success:** acceptance never strands programme data in a separate
tool, and published truth remains versioned.

## 5. Choose, work, and complete a volunteer shift

1. A department models a staffed position with time, place, briefing,
   qualifications, minimum coverage, breaks, supervision, and fallback.
2. Eligible volunteers see ranked opportunities based on their declared
   interests and constraints; Maru explains recommendations.
3. Selection or assignment checks all programme, travel, rehearsal, private
   busy-time, working-hour, rest, and qualification constraints.
4. Required onboarding and briefing become visible prerequisites.
5. Before the shift, the volunteer receives the current location, contact,
   briefing, accessibility notes relevant to the task, and material changes.
6. Check-in confirms presence. A late or absent response offers help and begins
   configured coverage escalation rather than public blame.
7. The lead records handover, completion, actual time where justified, and any
   follow-up.
8. Approved completion updates coverage, benefits, recognition, and private
   participation history.

**Proof of success:** shifts protect the volunteer's time and enable live
coverage decisions rather than merely filling spreadsheet cells.

The current first Shift journey implements explicit Position demand, current
Availability and exact-Position suitability, personal claim and withdrawal,
independent organizer confirmation/removal, transactional headcount and
overlap/rest checks, reasoned underfill locking, reopening, cancellation, and
post-end completion. Ranked interests, general qualifications, maximum hours,
travel, onboarding prerequisites in the Shift card, notifications, check-in,
late/absent escalation, actual time, handover acceptance, benefits, and
recognition remain later parts of this workflow rather than implied behavior.

## 6. Move a room during the live event

1. A venue problem is reported against the room and triaged by the responsible
   operator.
2. Maru identifies current and upcoming sessions, shifts, equipment, signs,
   accessibility routes, capacity, vendors, and responsible people.
3. The scheduler selects a suitable room or cancellation plan and sees new
   conflicts and setup time.
4. The change set proposes updated run sheets, assignments, public schedule,
   signage, maps, calendars, website/API data, and targeted messages.
5. Required operational owners approve or an authorized incident commander
   records an emergency override.
6. Publication occurs as one version. Each destination reports delivery state.
7. Hosts, room crew, access support, and other accountable recipients
   acknowledge or are escalated.
8. Event command sees completion gaps. Staff can answer attendees from the same
   source.

**Proof of success:** a room move is one coordinated operation, not eight
contradictory chat messages.

## 7. Handle a welfare, conduct, safety, or medical concern

1. A person uses an appropriate discreet channel or a staff member creates a
   report while addressing immediate safety through established procedures.
2. Routing policy sends it to an on-duty qualified role. Ordinary staff see
   only that a restricted matter has been handed over.
3. The responder records minimum necessary facts, consent, urgency, location,
   actions, and custody of supplied material.
4. Duty roles coordinate private tasks. Operational recipients receive
   redacted instructions, not diagnoses or allegations.
5. Break-glass access requires reason, is time-bound, alerts accountable
   reviewers, and enters an immutable audit trail.
6. Shift handover uses an explicit restricted summary and acknowledgement.
7. Closure records outcome category, follow-up, subject communication, external
   reference where appropriate, retention trigger, and post-event learning.

**Proof of success:** the right people coordinate quickly while curiosity,
accidental visibility, and indefinite retention are technically constrained.

## 8. Process a lost item

1. Staff record an item with category, safe description, found time/place,
   photo if appropriate, custody location, and restricted identifiers.
2. A claimant submits distinguishing details that are not exposed in the public
   listing.
3. Authorized staff compare claims, communicate through a case thread, and
   record release with identity verification appropriate to the item.
4. High-risk items such as credentials, keys, medication, or identity documents
   follow specialized handling and retention rules.
5. Unclaimed-item disposition is scheduled and evidenced.

**Proof of success:** a public-friendly search and a private custody process use
one record without leaking the fact that proves ownership.

## 9. Publish a normal or emergency announcement

1. The author selects a fact source or writes a canonical message, audience,
   validity window, priority, languages, and intended destinations.
2. Channel adapters produce previews with length, media, link, and formatting
   constraints.
3. Normal messages follow configured editorial approval. Emergency authority
   may use a shorter, separately audited path.
4. Publication creates immutable variants and queues idempotent delivery.
5. Success, remote identifier, retry, rate limit, and permanent failure are
   visible per channel.
6. Internal messages support acknowledgement when required.
7. Corrections link to the superseded announcement and update eligible durable
   surfaces without pretending the original external post disappeared.

**Proof of success:** Maru remains the canonical record even when a social
network is unavailable or removes its API.

## 10. Close, learn from, and archive an edition

1. Department closeout queues unresolved work, asset returns, expenses,
   supplier acceptance, incident follow-up, communications, and data review.
2. Finance, registration, commercial, inventory, access, and offline ledgers
   reconcile with exceptions assigned.
3. Temporary credentials, integrations, guest links, and elevated access
   expire; discrepancies block the access-close gate.
4. Retention policies remove or transform data whose operational purpose has
   ended. Legal holds are explicit and narrow.
5. Leads record outcomes, evidence, lessons, reusable templates, and proposed
   changes. Handover completeness is measurable.
6. Participants review permitted contributions and choose optional public
   visibility.
7. A signed archive manifest records important versions, counts, reports,
   policies, and integrity checks.
8. The edition becomes read-only. Authorized corrections form a visible
   amendment, never a silent rewrite.
9. The next edition can selectively adopt approved lessons and templates.

**Proof of success:** next year's team inherits knowledge rather than stale
assumptions, while participants retain a respectful history.

## Workflow invariants

Every consequential workflow must define:

- accountable owner and current state;
- valid transitions and authorization;
- deadline, edition time zone, and escalation;
- conversation and decision context;
- sensitive field and retention policy;
- idempotency and concurrency behavior;
- audit and user-visible timeline effects;
- notification and acknowledgement rules;
- degraded-mode behavior;
- cancellation, correction, and appeal path; and
- archive representation.

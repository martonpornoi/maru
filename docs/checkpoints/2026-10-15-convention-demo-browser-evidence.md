## Outcome and boundaries

The ordinary local launcher now opens a short trial with one ongoing and one
future fictional MaruCon. Each has three Announcements and three Guides: draft,
waiting review and approved. Separate Author, Reviewer and Reader accounts make
the relevant boundaries visible. Readers receive approved Guides only; the
existing Announcements staff permission also exposes drafts/history and is not
granted to them. Setup and platform accounts are kept off the practice page.

One captured Europe/Budapest date anchors both timelines. Reloading preserves
edits, dates and history. New invocations allocate a fresh disposable database;
reseed refuses existing data. Real foundation, acceptance, access and content
commands preserve exact authority provenance. The genuine restricted runtime
login prepares content after stopped maintenance activation. No production
schema, adoption profile, guard, permission boundary or acceptance gate changed.
The older v6 dataset remains available explicitly as the legacy scenario.

Public disposable practice credentials are generated in memory and shown only
by the explicitly enabled local page. Copy works while masked, normal sign-in
and CSRF remain required, and an explicit clear button replaces the clipboard.
Operating-system clipboard history/sync cannot be excluded by the browser and
is disclosed. Private acceptance fixtures and their stronger rules are unchanged.

## Browser and genuine-runtime evidence

Three loopback previews reached ready in 228, 226.1 and 230.7 seconds with the
actual restricted login. The warning about unavailable invitation encryption
remained visible and fail-closed; this prepared local trial does not need an
invitation-delivery worker or real delivery.

The assistant operated normal forms using only generated fictional credentials:

1. Ongoing Author copied a masked password, signed in, edited the welcome-desk
   Guide and requested review. The author could not approve that work.
2. A separate Reviewer approved the exact new Guide version and independently
   reviewed the pending sketch-meetup Announcement. Approval did not report
   external publication.
3. Ongoing Reader saw approved Guides and the newly approved text without private
   review notes, working history or settings.
4. Future Author found the three initial work states. Future Reviewer requested
   changes to the quiet-room Guide with private writer feedback. Future Reader
   saw only the initially approved room-finding Guide, downloaded its text through
   the browser, and the saved bytes contained the correct future edition/version
   without private feedback.
5. Future Reader's direct access to the ongoing Guides library and its own staff
   Announcements workspace returned opaque unavailable responses (HTTP 404).

The browser found a trial-page logout defect: `no-referrer` made the POST Origin
null and CSRF correctly refused it. The page now uses `same-origin`; repeated
normal logout POSTs returned 302 without changing CSRF validation. A prior
browser-tool download event wait stalled and was not treated as product evidence;
the later bounded download completed successfully.

The new start page had no horizontal overflow with a role expanded at measured
CSS widths 320, 390, 768, 958, 1024, 1280 and 1920. Phone and desktop views were
inspected, disclosure opened with Enter, Tab reached the labeled email field
with visible focus, and explicit clipboard clearing was verified. Browser
console inspection reported no errors/warnings for that page. Host display
scaling was accounted for by recording actual CSS viewport widths; it was not
changed. Temporary viewport overrides were reset.

## Disposal and remaining acceptance

The smoke preview and an expired one-hour browser preview removed their owned
resources. The final terminal interrupt ended the launcher before Python cleanup;
its exact container ID, generated name and ownership token were checked, then
only that disposable resource and its anonymous volume were removed. No demo
server/container remains. Other stopped fixtures, accounts, volumes, worktrees,
stashes and all earlier acceptance evidence were preserved.

This is a bounded synthetic rehearsal, not independent-human, specialist
accessibility, full 200-percent zoom/print, operational-owner or production
acceptance. It does not establish all-module UI coverage or close the wider
Knowledge document-governance scope. Exact-commit local certification, current
hosted PR gate/CodeQL and protected review/merge remain the next delivery steps.
